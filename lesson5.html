Part One: Writing Skills

1. What Is Cohesion?

Cohesion refers to the language that connects sentences and ideas. Cohesion helps the reader understand how one sentence relates to another.

A cohesive text uses tools such as:

  • Linking words
  • Pronouns
  • Reference words
  • Repetition of key ideas
  • Synonyms
  • Related vocabulary
  • Consistent verb tenses
  • Parallel structures
Example Without Cohesion

"Online learning is flexible. Online learning saves time. Online learning allows students to study from home. Online learning may feel isolating."

The sentences discuss the same topic, but the paragraph is repetitive.

Improved Cohesion

"Online learning is flexible and can save students a considerable amount of time. It allows them to study from home and organize lessons around other responsibilities. However, this form of education may sometimes feel isolating."

The improved version uses:
  • It to replace online learning
  • them to replace students
  • and to add information
  • however to show contrast
  • this form of education as a related expression

2. What Is Coherence?

Coherence means that ideas are: Clear, Logical, Relevant, Easy to follow, and Organized around one main point. A paragraph can contain many linking words but still be incoherent if its ideas are unrelated or badly organized.

Incoherent Paragraph

"Exercise is important because it improves health. However, many people enjoy watching films. Furthermore, sports shoes can be expensive. Therefore, walking can reduce stress. In addition, my neighbour has a dog."

The paragraph contains linking words, but the ideas do not follow a clear direction.

Coherent Paragraph

"Regular exercise can improve both physical and mental health. It strengthens the body and may reduce the risk of certain health problems. Exercise can also lower stress and improve sleep. For example, a daily walk may help a person feel calmer and more energetic. Therefore, regular physical activity can improve a person’s overall quality of life."

Coherent because: Every sentence supports main idea, logical order, relevant example, clear conclusion.

3. Cohesion and Coherence

Cohesion and coherence are connected, but they are not the same. A strong paragraph needs both.

CohesionCoherence
Connects languageConnects meaning
Uses linking words and reference wordsUses logical order and relevant ideas
Helps sentences flowHelps the complete text make sense
Focuses on language relationshipsFocuses on idea relationships
Cohesion: How sentences are connected.
Coherence: Whether the ideas make sense together.

4. Logical Organization

Before using linking words, the ideas must be in a sensible order. Common patterns include:

General to specific Cause to effect Problem to solution Past to present First step to final step Advantage to disadvantage Main point to example Opinion to supporting reason

General to Specific

"Technology has changed modern education. Students can now access digital books, online lessons, and interactive exercises. For example, a learner can review a recorded lesson several times."

The paragraph begins with a general statement and moves to a specific example.

Cause to Effect

"Many students delay their assignments until the final day. As a result, they feel stressed and may produce lower-quality work."

Problem to Solution

"Some learners understand grammar rules but cannot use them while speaking. One solution is to practise the same structure through short, repeated speaking tasks."

Chronological Order

"First, the student prepared an outline. Next, she wrote the first draft. After that, she checked the organization. Finally, she corrected the grammar and spelling."

5. Linking Words and Transitions (1)

Linking words show the relationship between ideas.

5.1 Addition

and, also, too, in addition, additionally, furthermore, moreover, another point is, not only… but also

"Reading improves vocabulary and develops concentration."

"She studies grammar. In addition, she practises speaking every day."

Formality: Neutral: and, also, another point is Formal: furthermore, moreover, additionally
Do not use highly formal linking words in every sentence.

5.2 Contrast

but, yet, however, although, even though, while, whereas, nevertheless, on the other hand, in contrast, despite, in spite of

But: Online learning is flexible, but it requires self-discipline.

Although: Although online learning is flexible, it requires self-discipline.

However: Online learning is flexible; however, it requires self-discipline. (Incorrect: Online learning is flexible, however it requires self-discipline.)

Despite (Followed by Noun/Gerund): Despite being tired, she continued. (Incorrect: Despite she was tired...)

5.3 Reason

because, since, as, because of, due to, one reason is that, the main reason is that

Because (Clause): She improved because she practised regularly.

Because of (Noun): The lesson was cancelled because of the snow. (Incorrect: because of it was snowing.)

Due to (Noun - Formal): The delay was due to technical problems.

5.4 Result

so, therefore, as a result, consequently, for this reason, this leads to, this results in, thus

So: She practised regularly, so her confidence improved.

Therefore/As a result: She practised regularly. Therefore, her confidence improved.

Cause: "She improved because she practised."
Result: "She practised, so she improved."

5. Linking Words and Transitions (2)

5.5 Examples

for example, for instance, such as, a clear example is, to illustrate

"Technology can support learning. For example, students can review lessons."

Such as (Inside sentence):

"Digital resources, such as videos, help students." (Avoid: Such as, videos can help...)

5.6 Sequence

first, next, then, after that, afterwards, meanwhile, finally, eventually

"First, read the question. Next, identify the topic. Finally, write the answer."

Finally vs Eventually:

Finally = last step. Eventually = after time/difficulty.

5.7 Comparison

similarly, likewise, both, in the same way, just as, compared with, equally

"Online and classroom learning both require active participation."

"Classroom learning provides direct interaction. Similarly, online lessons can include group discussions."

5.8 Condition

if, unless, as long as, provided that, in case

Unless = if not: You will not improve unless you practise.

As long as: You may use it as long as you follow the rules.

In case (Preparation): Take an umbrella in case it rains.

5.9 Clarification

in other words, that is, to put it differently, more specifically

"A coherent paragraph has a clear logical direction. In other words, the reader can easily understand how each sentence supports the main idea."

5.10 Conclusion

overall, in conclusion, to conclude, to sum up, in summary, for these reasons

"Overall, regular reading can improve both vocabulary and concentration."

"For these reasons, students should develop a consistent study routine."

6. Linking Words and Punctuation

Different linking words require different sentence structures.

FANBOYS

Use a comma when joining two independent clauses.

"I wanted to continue, but I was too tired."

Subordinating Conjunctions

Dependent first = comma. Dependent second = usually no comma.

"Although I was tired, I continued."

"I continued although I was tired."

Conjunctive Adverbs

Use a full stop or a semicolon.

"The course was difficult. However, it was useful."

"The course was difficult; however, it was useful."

7. Avoiding Linking-Word Overuse

A paragraph does not need a linking word at the beginning of every sentence.

Overused Version

"First, reading improves vocabulary. Furthermore, it develops concentration. Moreover, it increases knowledge. In addition, it introduces new ideas. Therefore, reading is useful."

Grammatically possible, but sounds mechanical.

Improved Version

"Reading improves vocabulary and develops concentration. It also increases general knowledge by introducing readers to new ideas and perspectives. For these reasons, regular reading is a valuable habit."

Uses fewer transitions but flows more naturally.

Important principle: Use a linking word only when it makes the relationship between ideas clearer.

8. Reference Words

Reference words point back to information already mentioned. They reduce repetition and improve cohesion.

it, they, this, that, these, those, such, this idea, this problem, this method, the former, the latter

Repetitive

"Social media helps people communicate. Social media also allows people to share information. Social media can become distracting."

Improved

"Social media helps people communicate and share information. However, it can become distracting."

Using This and These

"Many students leave assignments until the final day. This habit can create unnecessary stress."

Unclear Reference

"Sarah spoke to Emma after she finished the lesson." (Who finished? Sarah or Emma?)

Clearer Version

"After Sarah finished the lesson, she spoke to Emma."

9. Synonyms and Related Vocabulary

Repeating the same word too often makes writing weak. Use pronouns, synonyms, related expressions, and general/specific terms.

Repetitive

"Online education is flexible. Online education is convenient. Online education saves time."

Improved

"Online education is flexible and convenient. This learning method can also save students time."

Useful Variation Examples

Students: learners, pupils, participants
Important: essential, significant, valuable
Problem: issue, difficulty, challenge
Improve: develop, strengthen, enhance

Note: Synonyms do not always have exactly the same meaning. Choose a word that fits the context.

10. Lexical Cohesion

Lexical cohesion means using vocabulary from the same topic area to maintain a clear topic.

Topic: Education student, teacher, lesson, classroom, assignment, learning, assessment, curriculum
Weak: Education is important. Cars are expensive. Teachers help people. Technology changes quickly. (Unrelated topics)
Improved: Education is important because it gives learners knowledge... Teachers guide students through lessons, assignments, and assessments.

11. Parallel Structure

Parallel structure improves clarity and rhythm.

Incorrect She enjoys reading books, to watch films, and travelling. Correct She enjoys reading books, watching films, and travelling.

12. Consistent Verb Tenses

Unnecessary tense changes make writing difficult to follow.

Inconsistent Last week, I attended a workshop and learn several new techniques. The teacher explains everything clearly. Correct Last week, I attended a workshop and learned several new techniques. The teacher explained everything clearly.

Exception: A tense change is correct when the time/meaning changes. (e.g. "Last week, I attended a workshop. I now use the techniques...")

13. Weak and Strong Cohesion Comparison

Weak Paragraph

"Time management is important. Time management helps students. Students have assignments. Students become stressed. Time management reduces stress."

Repetitive, disjointed, lacks flow.
Strong Paragraph

"Time management is important because it helps students organize their assignments and avoid last-minute work. Without a clear plan, they may become overwhelmed when several deadlines occur at the same time. This pressure can reduce concentration and lower the quality of their work. Therefore, managing time effectively can improve both performance and confidence."

Why Better: Uses pronouns, reference words ("This pressure"), cause-and-effect language, clear logical order, varied vocabulary, concluding sentence.

14. Weak and Strong Coherence Comparison

Weak Paragraph

"Learning English can improve career opportunities. English has many grammar rules. Some people enjoy travelling. International companies communicate in English. Watching films is relaxing."

Problems: Ideas not connected, some sentences do not support main point, paragraph changes direction.
Strong Paragraph

"Learning English can improve career opportunities because many international companies use it for communication. Employees may need English to write emails, attend meetings, or speak with clients from other countries. For example, a person with strong English skills may qualify for positions that require international communication. Therefore, improving English can increase a person’s professional options."

15. Common Cohesion and Coherence Problems

15.1 Repetition

Reading is useful because reading improves vocabulary, and reading develops knowledge.

Reading is useful because it improves vocabulary and develops general knowledge.

15.4 Unclear Pronouns

Unclear: The teacher spoke to the student because she was worried.

(Who was worried?)

Clearer: The teacher spoke to the student because the student appeared worried.

15.2 Incorrect Linking Word

She was tired. Therefore, she continued working.

Therefore shows a result, but the relationship is contrast.

She was tired. However, she continued working.

15.3 Too Many Linking Words

Weak: Firstly, exercise is useful. Secondly, it reduces stress. Moreover, it helps sleep. Furthermore, it gives energy. Therefore, exercise is useful.

Improved: Exercise can reduce stress, improve sleep, and increase energy. Therefore, regular physical activity can support a healthier lifestyle.

15.5 Unrelated Details

Weak: Online learning is flexible. Students can study from home. My brother bought a new computer. Learners can also review recorded lessons.

The sentence about the brother is not relevant.

15.6 Illogical Order

Weak: Finally, write the conclusion. First, choose the topic. After that, check the grammar. Next, create an outline.

Order must be logical (First, Next, After writing, Finally).

15.7 Sudden Topic Change

Weak: Reading improves vocabulary. It also develops concentration. Social media can be distracting.

The final sentence changes the topic.

Practice 1: Choose the Correct Linking Word

Choose the best option to complete the sentence logically.

1. She practised regularly; __________, her pronunciation improved.

a. however
b. therefore
c. although

2. __________ the task was difficult, she completed it.

a. Although
b. Therefore
c. Because of

3. The lesson was useful. __________, it was very long.

a. For example
b. However
c. Therefore

4. Students can use resources __________ videos and online dictionaries.

a. therefore
b. because
c. such as

Note: Choosing the correct linking word depends on understanding the logical relationship (Addition, Contrast, Reason, Result, etc.) between the connected ideas.
