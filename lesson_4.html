Lesson 4

Paragraph Types, Opinions, and Storytelling

Lesson Goals

Recognize the main paragraph types

Choose the correct paragraph type for a topic

Understand how structure changes by purpose

Write descriptive, narrative, opinion, compare-and-contrast, and cause-and-effect paragraphs

Use suitable language for each type

Avoid mixing different paragraph purposes

Express opinions clearly

Agree and disagree politely

Give balanced answers

Use STAR and 5W1H for storytelling

Part One: Writing Skills

1. What Is a Paragraph Type?

A paragraph type is determined by its purpose.

Before writing, ask:

  • Am I describing something?
  • Am I telling a story?
  • Am I giving an opinion?
  • Am I comparing two things?
  • Am I explaining causes and results?

The purpose determines:

  • The structure
  • The vocabulary
  • The tense
  • The supporting details
  • The conclusion

The 5 Main Paragraph Types in this Lesson:

1. Descriptive 2. Narrative 3. Opinion 4. Compare and contrast 5. Cause and effect

2. Descriptive Paragraphs

A descriptive paragraph describes a person, place, object, experience, or atmosphere. Its purpose is to help the reader imagine the subject clearly.

Main Features

  • - Clear physical details
  • - Adjectives
  • - Sensory language
  • - Spatial order
  • - Specific examples
  • - A general impression

Sensory Language

Sight, Sound, Smell, Taste, Touch

  • "Warm yellow light"
  • "Soft music could be heard"
  • "Smelled of fresh coffee"
  • "Felt smooth and cold"

Spatial Order

Where things are located

on the left, on the right, in the centre, next to, behind, in front of, above, below, near, in the corner.

Weak Descriptive Paragraph

"My classroom is nice. It has tables and chairs. The walls are nice. The classroom is big. I like it."

Problems: 'Nice' is repeated. Details too general. No clear image. No sensory language. Final sentence is weak.

Strong Descriptive Paragraph

"My classroom is a bright and welcoming space. Large windows on the left allow natural light to fill the room, while colourful educational posters cover the walls. The desks are arranged in a semicircle so that students can see one another during discussions. A small reading corner stands near the back, with soft cushions and a wooden bookshelf. Overall, the classroom feels comfortable, organized, and suitable for active learning."

Why Stronger: Clear image, follows spatial order, precise adjectives, ends with overall impression.

3. Narrative Paragraphs

A narrative paragraph tells a personal experience, event, memory, problem, or short story. It usually follows chronological order.

Main Features

  • - Past tenses
  • - Time expressions
  • - Characters & Setting
  • - Main event
  • - Problem or turning point
  • - Result & Reflection

Basic Structure

  1. Background
  2. Beginning of the event
  3. Main event or problem
  4. Reaction
  5. Result
  6. Reflection

Time Expressions

one day, at first, while, when, suddenly, a few minutes later, after that, eventually, in the end, looking back.

Weak Narrative Paragraph

"I went to school. I had a presentation. I was nervous. I spoke. It was good. I went home."

Problems: Sentences too basic. No event development. Feelings not explained. No specific example. Ending is weak.

Strong Narrative Paragraph

"Last year, I had to give an important presentation in front of my class. At first, I felt extremely nervous because I had never spoken to such a large group before. While I was waiting for my turn, I reviewed my notes and tried to control my breathing. When I finally started speaking, my voice was slightly shaky, but I gradually became more confident. By the end of the presentation, I was able to answer the questions clearly. Looking back, the experience taught me that preparation can reduce fear and improve confidence."

Why Stronger: Clear sequence, correct past tenses, includes feelings/actions, shows development, ends with a lesson.

4. Opinion Paragraphs

An opinion paragraph expresses a personal position, belief, judgement, or recommendation. The opinion must be supported with reasons and examples.

Basic Opinion Structure

  1. State the opinion
  2. Give a reason
  3. Explain the reason
  4. Give an example
  5. Add another reason if needed
  6. Conclude

Useful Opinion Language

  • - In my opinion...
  • - I believe that...
  • - From my perspective...
  • - It seems to me that...
  • - I would argue that...
  • - Personally, I think...
Weak Opinion Paragraph

"I think online learning is better. It is good and easy. Many students like it. I like it too."

Problems: Opinion not supported clearly. 'Good' and 'easy' are too general. No specific example. Conclusion repeats opinion.

Strong Opinion Paragraph

"In my opinion, online learning is particularly useful for students with busy schedules. It allows them to attend lessons from different locations and organize study time around work or family responsibilities. For example, a working student may join an evening class without spending time travelling to a school. Although online learning requires self-discipline, its flexibility makes education more accessible. For these reasons, I believe it is an effective option for many learners."

Why Stronger: Opinion is clear, reason is specific, example is relevant, includes a balanced point, conclusion returns to opinion.

5. Compare-and-Contrast Paragraphs

Explains similarities, differences, advantages, disadvantages, and a final judgement.

Compare and Contrast Language

Similarity

both, similarly, likewise, in the same way, also

Difference

however, whereas, while, unlike, in contrast, on the other hand

Two Common Structures

Point-by-Point Structure: Compare both subjects according to one point at a time. (e.g. Flexibility, Cost, Interaction).
*Often clearer for one paragraph.

Block Structure: Discuss the first subject, then the second subject.

Weak Paragraph

"Online classes are different from classroom classes. Online classes are flexible. Classroom classes are good. They are both useful."

Problems: Comparison incomplete. Language too general. Similarities and differences not clearly organized. No conclusion.

Strong Paragraph

"Online and classroom education can both support effective learning, but they offer different experiences. Online lessons provide greater flexibility because students can attend from different locations. Classroom lessons, however, allow more direct interaction with teachers and classmates. While online learning may suit independent students, classroom learning may be better for those who need immediate support. Overall, the best choice depends on the learner’s needs and study habits."

6. Cause-and-Effect Paragraphs

Explains why something happens and what results it produces (One cause/many effects, Many causes/one effect, or a chain).

Language

Cause: because, since, due to, because of, one reason is, results from

Effect: therefore, as a result, consequently, this leads to, this causes, one result is

7. Choosing the Correct Type

The same topic can be written in different ways. The purpose must be clear before writing.

Topic: A City

  • Descriptive: Describe appearance, atmosphere, places.
  • Narrative: Tell a story about something that happened there.
  • Opinion: Explain whether it is a good place to live.
  • Compare/Contrast: Compare it with another city.
  • Cause/Effect: Explain why it's crowded or how tourism affects it.
Weak Cause-and-Effect

"Students feel stressed because they have work. Stress is bad. It causes problems."

Problems: Causes unclear, effects not explained, vocabulary too general, underdeveloped.

Strong Cause-and-Effect

"Poor time management is a common cause of student stress. When students delay assignments, several tasks may become urgent at the same time. As a result, they may sleep less, lose concentration, and produce lower-quality work. This pressure can also reduce motivation because students begin to feel overwhelmed. Therefore, planning tasks in advance can help prevent unnecessary academic stress."

8. Do Not Mix Paragraph Types Carelessly

A paragraph may include small elements from another type, but one purpose should remain dominant.

Weak Mixed Paragraph

"My city is beautiful and crowded. Last week, I went to the market. Cities are better than villages. Traffic causes stress. I like the old buildings."

Problems: Describes city, tells story, gives opinion, cause/effect. No central purpose.

Improved Descriptive Version

"My city is known for its historic buildings and lively streets. In the old town, narrow stone roads pass between traditional houses and small local shops. During the day, the markets are crowded and full of conversation, while the evenings are quieter and more peaceful. Although traffic can be heavy in the newer areas, the historic centre maintains a unique atmosphere. Overall, the city combines traditional character with modern daily life."

9. Paragraph Planning Through Questions

Descriptive What am I describing? General impression? What does it look like? Where are details? Sensory info? Final impression?
Narrative Who? Where? When? What happened first? Main event? How did I react? Result? What did I learn?
Opinion What is my opinion? Why? Strongest reason? Explanation? Example? Alternative view? Conclusion?
Compare-and-Contrast Two subjects? Common? Differences? Most important points? More suitable? Final conclusion?
Cause-and-Effect What happened? Why? Main causes? Effects? Most important effect? What can be done?

10. Model Paragraphs by Type

Descriptive Model

"The library is one of the quietest and most comfortable places in the school. Tall bookshelves cover the walls, and large windows allow natural light to enter the room. In the centre, several wooden tables provide space for students to read and study. The only sounds are turning pages and quiet conversations. Overall, the library creates a peaceful environment for concentration."

Narrative Model

"One morning, I arrived at work and discovered that the internet was not working. I had prepared an online activity, so I had to change the lesson immediately. At first, I felt stressed, but I decided to use the whiteboard and printed materials instead. The students participated actively, and the lesson went better than I expected. This experience taught me the importance of having an alternative plan."

Opinion Model

"I believe students should receive regular homework, but the amount should be reasonable. Homework allows learners to review lessons and practise independently. For example, a short writing task can help a student use new vocabulary after class. However, excessive homework may cause stress and reduce motivation. Therefore, homework is most effective when it is purposeful and manageable."

Compare-and-Contrast Model

"Working alone and working in a team both have advantages. Working alone allows a person to make decisions independently and concentrate without interruption. Teamwork, however, gives people an opportunity to exchange ideas and divide responsibilities. While independent work may be faster for simple tasks, teamwork can be more effective for complex projects. Therefore, the best approach depends on the type of work."

Cause-and-Effect Model

"Excessive phone use before bedtime can negatively affect sleep. Bright screens may prevent the brain from relaxing, while messages and videos keep users mentally active. As a result, people may fall asleep later and feel tired the next morning. Poor sleep can also reduce concentration during work or study. Therefore, limiting screen time before bed can improve sleep quality."

11. Common Problems in Paragraph Types

Descriptive Paragraph Problems

  • Using too many general adjectives
  • Listing details without order
  • Giving unrelated information
  • Describing everything equally
  • Repeating beautiful, nice, or good

Narrative Paragraph Problems

  • Changing tenses unnecessarily
  • Giving events in the wrong order
  • Adding too many unimportant details
  • Ending without a result or reflection
  • Using only then to connect events

Opinion Paragraph Problems

  • Giving an opinion without support
  • Repeating I think
  • Using emotional language instead of reasons
  • Ignoring other perspectives
  • Giving unrelated examples

Compare-and-Contrast Problems

  • Describing one subject more than the other
  • Comparing unrelated points / Using only but
  • Forgetting the final judgement
  • Mixing similarities & differences without order

Cause-and-Effect Problems

  • Confusing cause and result
  • Giving effects without explaining the cause
  • Giving too many causes without development
  • Using incorrect connectors
  • Adding solutions when paragraph is only about causes

Part Two: Speaking Skills

12. Expressing Opinions Clearly

A strong opinion answer should include: 1. Clear opinion, 2. Reason, 3. Explanation, 4. Example, 5. Conclusion.

Weak Opinion Answer

Q: Is social media useful?

"Yes, it is useful because people use it."

Problems: Reason repeats question. No explanation. No example. Too general.

Strong Opinion Answer

"I believe social media can be useful when people use it responsibly. It allows users to communicate, share information, and maintain relationships across long distances. For example, families living in different countries can stay in contact through messages and video calls. However, excessive use can become distracting. Overall, social media is beneficial when it is used in a balanced way."

13. Giving Opinions (Useful Expressions)

In my opinion... I believe that... From my perspective... Personally, I think... It seems to me that... I would argue that... My view is that...

Rule: Use one clear opinion phrase. Do not repeat several of them in the same answer.

14, 15 & 16. Agreeing and Disagreeing

14. Complete Agreement

  • - I completely agree.
  • - I feel the same way.
  • - That is exactly how I see it.
  • - I think that is a very good point.
  • - I could not agree more.
"I completely agree because regular reading can improve both vocabulary and concentration."

15. Partial Agreement

Shows balance.

  • - I agree to some extent, but...
  • - That may be true; however...
  • - I understand the point, although...
  • - While I agree that..., I also think...
  • - I partly agree because...
"I agree to some extent that online learning is convenient, but it may not provide enough interaction for every student."

16. Disagreeing Politely

Avoid: "You are wrong."

  • - I see the situation differently.
  • - I am not sure I completely agree.
  • - I understand your point, but...
  • - Another way of looking at it is...
  • - That may be true in some cases, but...
  • - I would take a different view.
"I understand your point, but I think classroom learning offers stronger communication between students and teachers."

Balanced Opinion Techniques

17. Advantages–Disadvantages–Opinion Technique

Useful for balanced questions. Contains: 1. One advantage, 2. One disadvantage, 3. Personal opinion.

Q: What do you think about working from home?

[Advantage] One advantage of working from home is that employees save commuting time and can organize their schedules more flexibly. [Disadvantage] However, a disadvantage is that they may feel isolated or have difficulty separating work from personal life. [Opinion] Personally, I think working from home is effective when employees have a suitable environment and regular communication with colleagues.

18. Opinion–Alternative View–Conclusion Technique

Helps create balanced answers. Contains: 1. Main opinion, 2. Alternative view, 3. Final conclusion.

Q: Is university education necessary for success?

I believe university education can create valuable opportunities because it provides specialized knowledge and qualifications. However, some people become successful through practical experience, professional training, or entrepreneurship. Overall, university is an important path, but it is not the only possible route to success.

19. Compare–Contrast–Conclude Technique

Useful when comparing two options. Contains: 1. Introduce options, 2. Similarities/Differences, 3. Conclusion.

Q: Is it better to study online or in a classroom?

Online and classroom learning can both be effective, but they offer different advantages. Online learning is more flexible, while classroom learning provides more direct interaction. Students who are independent may prefer online lessons, whereas those who need immediate support may benefit from classroom learning. Personally, I think the best choice depends on the learner’s personality and responsibilities.

Storytelling Techniques

20. Storytelling with STAR

Useful for challenges, achievements, problems, work experiences, job interviews.

  • S - Situation (Background)
  • T - Task (Responsibility)
  • A - Action (What you did)
  • R - Result (Outcome)
"During a group project, two members disagreed [S]. My responsibility was to help the group continue [T]. I listened and suggested combining ideas [A]. As a result, the team reached agreement and completed the project [R]."

21. Storytelling with 5W1H

Helps you add important details to personal stories or events.

Who? What? Where? When? Why? How?
"One of my most memorable days [Why] was my final presentation [What] last year [When]. My classmates [Who] were present at the university [Where]. I felt nervous at first but became confident [How]."

22. STAR or 5W1H?

Use STAR when: question asks about a challenge, solving a problem, achievement, responsibility, taking action.
Use 5W1H when: describing an event, personal story, needing details, setting a clear scene. (They can also be used together!)

23 & 24. Weak vs Strong Storytelling

Weak Story

"I had a problem at work. I talked to someone. It was solved."

Problems: Situation unclear. Responsibility missing. Action too general. Result not explained.

Strong Story

"Last month, a customer contacted our office because an important document had not arrived. I was responsible for checking the situation and providing an update. I contacted the delivery company, found the tracking information, and explained the delay to the customer. As a result, the document arrived the following day, and the customer thanked us for the support."

24. Storytelling Language

Beginning
  • One day...
  • Last year...
  • A few months ago...
  • I remember a time when...
  • The situation began when...
Sequence
  • At first...
  • Then...
  • After that...
  • A few minutes later...
  • Meanwhile...
  • Eventually...
Unexpected / Result
  • Suddenly... / To my surprise...
  • As a result...
  • In the end...
  • Eventually...
  • Finally...
Reflection
  • Looking back...
  • This experience taught me...
  • I realized that...
  • If I faced the situation again...

25 & 26. Self-Assessment Checklists

Speaking Self-Assessment

Opinions

Did I state opinion clearly? Reason? Explanation? Example? Consider another view? Conclude clearly?

Agreeing and Disagreeing

Did I respond politely? Explain why? Avoid direct/emotional language? Add useful point?

Storytelling

Explain situation? Order clear? Relevant details? Explain action? Explain result? Finish with lesson/reflection?

Writing Self-Editing

Paragraph Type

Purpose clear? Correct structure? Avoided mixing unrelated purposes?

Specific Types

  • Descriptive: Clear image? Specific details? Spatial order?
  • Narrative: Event order clear? Past tenses consistent? Result/reflection?
  • Opinion: Opinion clear? Supported with reasons/examples? Linked conclusion?
  • Compare/Contrast: Compared same points? Discussed fairly? Final judgement?
  • Cause/Effect: Causes/effects clear? Connectors correct? Explained relationship?
