Lesson 1

Building Strong Sentences and Extending Spoken Answers

Lesson Goals

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Recognize the main parts of speech
  • Choose the correct word form
  • Use articles and pronouns correctly
  • Organize adjectives in a natural order
  • Understand the basic functions of verbs and adverbs
  • Use common prepositions and conjunctions
  • Build complete English sentences
  • Expand basic sentences with useful details
  • Correct common sentence problems
  • Use ARE and PREP to develop spoken answers
PART ONE: WRITING SKILLS

1. Knowing a Word

Knowing a word means more than knowing its translation.

You should also know:

  • Its part of speech
  • Its pronunciation
  • Its different forms
  • Its position in a sentence
  • The words commonly used with it
  • Whether it is formal, informal, or neutral

Example: Success

2. Parts of Speech

The main parts of speech are:

Nouns Articles Pronouns Adjectives Verbs Adverbs Prepositions Conjunctions Determiners

2.1 Nouns

A noun names: A person, An animal, A place, An object or thing, An activity, A feeling, An idea.

Examples

CategoryExamples
Personteacher, student, doctor
Animalcat, horse, bird
Placeschool, city, Türkiye
Objectphone, book, table
Activityreading, swimming, travelling
Feelinghappiness, fear, confidence
Ideafreedom, education, success

Common and Proper Nouns

Common nouns

General names:

teacher, country, university, language

Proper nouns

Specific names:

Osama, Türkiye, Mardin, English, Mardin Artuklu University

Proper nouns begin with a capital letter.

Concrete and Abstract Nouns

Concrete nouns

Things that can normally be experienced through the senses:

apple, chair, music, perfume

Abstract nouns

Ideas, feelings, qualities, or conditions:

knowledge, happiness, confidence, freedom

Countable and Uncountable Nouns

Countable nouns

They can be counted.

one book, two books, a student, several students

Uncountable nouns

They are not normally counted individually.

information, advice, furniture, homework, research, knowledge, money

IncorrectShe gave me many useful informations.
CorrectShe gave me a lot of useful information.
IncorrectHe gave me an advice.
CorrectHe gave me some advice.
Important exception: Words such as information, advice, furniture, homework, and research do not normally take a plural -s.

Gerunds: Verb + ing Used as a Noun

A verb ending in -ing can function as a noun. This is called a gerund.

  • Reading improves vocabulary.
  • Swimming is good exercise.
  • She enjoys travelling.

However, not every -ing form is a noun.

Gerund

Speaking English requires practice.

Part of a continuous verb

She is speaking English.

The form is the same, but its function is different.

2.2 Articles

The English articles are: a, an, the. Sometimes, no article is used. This is called the zero article.

A and An

Use a/an with:

  • One singular countable noun
  • Something non-specific
  • Something mentioned for the first time
  • A profession

Examples

  • I bought a book.
  • She is a teacher.
  • He needs an answer.

A or An Depends on Sound

Use a before a consonant sound:

  • a student
  • a university
  • a European country
  • a useful idea

Use an before a vowel sound:

  • an apple
  • an hour
  • an honest person
  • an interesting lesson
Important exception: Choose a or an according to pronunciation, not only according to the first written letter.

The

Use the when:

  • The noun is specific
  • The listener knows which noun you mean
  • The noun was mentioned before
  • There is only one in the situation
  • You use a superlative
  • You refer to certain geographical names

Mentioned before: I bought a book.
The book is about communication.

Specific noun: Please close the door.

Unique things: the sun, the moon, the internet

Superlatives: She is the best student in the class.

Certain geographical names: the United States, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, the Nile, the Mediterranean Sea, the Alps

Zero Article

No article is normally used with:

  • General plural nouns
  • General uncountable nouns
  • Languages
  • School subjects
  • Most countries and cities
  • Meals in a general sense

Examples

  • Books improve vocabulary.
  • Education is important.
  • She speaks English.
  • He studies psychology.
  • They live in Türkiye.
  • We had breakfast at eight.

General and Specific Meaning

General: I like coffee.

Specific: I did not like the coffee at that restaurant.

2.3 Pronouns (1 of 2)

Pronouns replace nouns and prevent unnecessary repetition.

Repetitive Sarah joined the course because Sarah wanted Sarah’s speaking to improve.
Improved Sarah joined the course because she wanted her speaking to improve.

Subject pronouns perform the action.

2. Object Pronouns

Object pronouns receive the action or appear after a preposition.

SubjectObject
I, you, he, she, it, we, theyme, you, him, her, it, us, them
Examples: The teacher helped me. | I spoke to him. | She invited them.

2.3 Pronouns (2 of 2)

6. Demonstrative Pronouns

this, that, these, those

  • This is useful.
  • That belongs to me.
  • These are new.
  • Those are expensive.

When they replace a noun, they are pronouns.
This is useful.

When they come before a noun, they are determiners.
This lesson is useful.

7. Relative Pronouns

Relative pronouns connect extra information to a noun.

  • Who: Used mainly for people.
    The teacher who helped me was patient.
  • Whom: Used for people as the object, mainly formal.
    The person whom I contacted replied. (In everyday English, who is often used instead).
  • Whose: Shows possession.
    The student whose project won received an award.
  • Which: Used for animals and things.
    The book which I bought is useful.
  • That: Often used for people, animals, or things.
    The course that I joined was helpful.

8. Interrogative Pronouns

Used to ask questions: who, whom, whose, what, which

Examples: Who called you? | What happened? | Which do you prefer?

9. Indefinite Pronouns

They refer to people or things without identifying them specifically.

People: someone, somebody, anyone, anybody, everyone, everybody, no one, nobody
Things & Others: something, anything, everything, nothing, each, either, neither, both, several, many, few, all, some, none
  • Someone called you.
  • Everyone is ready.
  • I did not hear anything.
  • Neither was suitable.
Important agreement rule: Everyone, everybody, someone, somebody, nobody, and no one are grammatically singular.
Everyone is ready.

Singular they is common when gender is unknown or unimportant.
Someone left their phone here.

10. Reciprocal Pronouns

each other, one another

Examples: The students helped each other. | The team members respect one another.

2.4 Adjectives

Adjectives describe nouns or pronouns. They may describe: Opinion, Size, Age, Shape, Colour, Origin, Material, Purpose, Quality, Number.

Position of Adjectives

Before a noun

an interesting lesson, a new laptop

After a linking verb

The lesson was interesting. The student became confident.

Common linking verbs include: be, seem, become, feel, look, sound, appear.

Common Adjective Types

Descriptive: beautiful, difficult, intelligent, useful

Proper: (From proper nouns) Turkish food, English literature, European history

Compound: well-organized, time-consuming, part-time, five-minute.
It was a well-organized lesson. We had a ten-minute break.

Important rule: A compound adjective before a noun is often hyphenated.
Incorrect: a ten-minutes presentation Correct: a ten-minute presentation

Adjective Order: OSASCOMP

A useful way to remember adjective order is: OSASCOMP ("Osama's Company")

LetterCategoryExamples
OOpinionbeautiful, useful, interesting
SSizelarge, small, tall
AAgeold, new, young
SShaperound, square, rectangular
CColourblack, white, red
OOriginTurkish, Italian, Yemeni
MMaterialwooden, cotton, metal
PPurposesleeping, cooking, writing
Example: a beautiful old Italian wooden table
Quantity comes first: three beautiful old wooden chairs
Incorrect a wooden beautiful old table
Correct a beautiful old wooden table
Important exception: OSASCOMP is a strong general pattern, but writers may sometimes change the order for emphasis or style. It is the safest order for normal communication.

-ed and -ing Adjectives

-ed adjectives

Describe how someone feels.

bored, interested, excited, confused, tired

-ing adjectives

Describe the person, thing, or situation that causes the feeling.

boring, interesting, exciting, confusing, tiring

Examples:
I was bored because the film was boring.
The student felt confused because the instructions were confusing.

2.5 Verbs

Verbs express: Actions, States, Processes, Experiences, Changes. A complete English sentence normally needs a verb.

Action Verbs

write, speak, study, travel, improve

Examples:
She writes every day.
They travelled last summer.

State Verbs

State verbs describe opinions, emotions, possession, or mental states.

Common state verbs: know, understand, believe, prefer, need, want, love, hate, own, belong

Examples: I understand the lesson. She prefers online learning.

State verbs are not normally used in continuous forms.

Incorrect: I am knowing the answer.
Correct: I know the answer.

Important exception: Some state verbs can be continuous when their meaning changes.
I think he is right. (This means believe.)
I am thinking about the problem. (This means actively considering.)

Linking Verbs

Linking verbs connect the subject to a description.

Common linking verbs: be, become, seem, appear, feel, look, sound, smell, taste

Examples: She is confident. The plan seems effective. The food smells delicious.

An adjective normally follows a linking verb.

Incorrect: The food smells deliciously.
Correct: The food smells delicious.

Auxiliary Verbs

The main auxiliary verbs are: be, have, do

They help form: Tenses, Questions, Negatives, Passive structures

Examples: She is studying. They have completed the task. Do you understand? He does not agree.

Modal Verbs

Common modal verbs: can, could, may, might, must, should, will, would

Examples: She can speak English. You should practise regularly. They might arrive later.

A base verb follows a modal.

Incorrect: She can speaks English.
Correct: She can speak English.

Transitive and Intransitive Verbs

Transitive verbs (They need an object.)

She explained the problem. He opened the door.

Intransitive verbs (They do not need an object.)

The baby slept. They arrived.

Some verbs can be both: She runs every morning. vs She runs a company.

2.6 Adverbs

Adverbs may describe: A verb, An adjective, Another adverb, A complete sentence.

Adverbs of Manner

They describe how an action happens.

carefully, confidently, slowly, clearly, successfully

Examples: She spoke confidently. He explained the idea clearly.

Adverbs of Frequency

always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never

Before main verb: She usually studies in the evening.

After verb 'be': She is usually prepared.

Adverbs of Time

today, yesterday, soon, already, recently, later

Examples: I completed the task yesterday. She has already arrived.

Adverbs of Place

here, there, outside, upstairs, abroad

Examples: They waited outside. She wants to study abroad.

Adverbs of Degree

very, extremely, quite, rather, slightly, completely, almost

Examples: The lesson was extremely useful. He was slightly nervous.

Sentence Adverbs

They comment on the complete sentence.

fortunately, unfortunately, honestly, surprisingly, probably

Examples: Fortunately, nobody was injured. Surprisingly, the task was easy.

Adjective or Adverb?

Adjective: She is a careful writer.
(Describes the person)

Adverb: She writes carefully.
(Describes the action)

Important Exceptions

Not every adverb ends in -ly: fast, hard, late, early, well.

Some words ending in -ly are adjectives: friendly, lovely, lonely, lively.

Hard: works with effort
Hardly: almost does not work
Late: not on time
Lately: recently

2.7 Prepositions

Prepositions show relationships involving: Time, Place, Direction, Method, Topic, Cause, Relationships.

Prepositions of Time

At (Specific times): at 7:00, at midnight, at lunchtime

On (Days and dates): on Monday, on 15 July, on my birthday

In (Months, years, seasons...): in July, in 2026, in summer, in the twenty-first century

Prepositions of Place

At (Point/general location): at school, at home, at the door

In (Enclosed space, city, country): in the room, in Mardin, in Türkiye

On (A surface): on the table, on the wall, on the floor

Prepositions of Topic

about, on, regarding, concerning

Examples: We spoke about the course. She gave a presentation on education. I am writing regarding your application.

Prepositions of Direction & Method

Direction: to, into, onto, toward, through, across, along, from

She walked into the room. They travelled from Mardin to Istanbul. He walked across the street.

Method: by, with, through

She improved by practising regularly. He opened the box with a knife. They communicated through email.

Dependent Prepositions

Many words are followed by specific prepositions.

Adj + prep: interested in, good at, responsible for, afraid of, worried about, proud of
Verb/Noun + prep: depend on, listen to, belong to, apply for / reason for, solution to
Learn the complete combination. Do not memorize only: interested. Memorize: interested in.

Phrasal Verbs

A phrasal verb combines a verb and a particle.

Examples: find out, give up, look after, put off, carry on, deal with, look into, come across

Example: She looked after the child. (This means she cared for the child.)

Important learning note: Phrasal verbs should be memorized and practised as complete expressions. Their meanings are not always clear from the individual words.

2.8 Conjunctions

Conjunctions connect words, phrases, and clauses.

Level 1: FANBOYS

The coordinating conjunctions are:

Ffor (reason)Yyet (contrast)
Aand (addition)Sso (result)
Nnor (neg. alt)
Bbut (contrast)
Oor (choice)

Examples: She studied grammar, and she completed the exercises. The task was difficult, but she completed it.

Comma Rule: Use a comma before FANBOYS when it joins two complete sentences. (I wanted to attend, but I was feeling unwell.) No comma is usually needed when it only joins words. (She bought pens and notebooks.)

Level 3: Correlative Conjunctions

They work in pairs: both…and, either…or, neither…nor, not only…but also, whether…or

Examples: She is both intelligent and hardworking. The course improves not only writing but also speaking.

Parallel Structure: The forms after paired conjunctions should match.
Incorrect: She enjoys both reading and to write.
Correct: She enjoys both reading and writing.

Level 2: Subordinating Conjunctions

  • Reason: because, since, as
  • Contrast: although, even though, while, whereas
  • Time: when, while, before, after, until, as soon as
  • Condition: if, unless, provided that, as long as
  • Purpose: so that, in order that

Examples: I practise every day because I want to improve. Although the task was difficult, she completed it.

Comma Position: When the dependent clause comes first, use a comma. (Although I was tired, I continued working.) When it comes second, a comma is usually unnecessary. (I continued working although I was tired.)

Level 4: Conjunctive Adverbs

Examples: however, therefore, moreover, nevertheless, consequently, furthermore, otherwise, meanwhile

Correct: The task was difficult; however, she completed it.

Correct: The task was difficult. However, she completed it.

Incorrect: The task was difficult, however she completed it.

A comma alone cannot normally join two complete sentences with however.

2.9 Determiners

Determiners come before nouns. They show:

  • Which noun
  • Whose noun
  • How many
  • How much
  • Whether the noun is specific

Articles, possessive words, demonstratives, and many quantity words are types of determiners.

Demonstrative Determiners

In this lesson, the main focus is on: this, that, these, those

This

One person or thing near the speaker:
This book is useful.

That

One person or thing farther away:
That building is old.

These

More than one near the speaker:
These exercises are easy.

Those

More than one farther away:
Those students are waiting.

Pronoun or Determiner?

Pronoun

This is useful.

It replaces the noun.

Determiner

This lesson is useful.

It comes before the noun.

Weak and Strong Vocabulary

Weak

"She gave a good presentation, and she spoke in a good way. The audience thought it was good."

Problems

  • Good is repeated.
  • "Spoke in a good way" is unnatural.
  • The vocabulary is too general.
  • The strengths are not explained.
Strong

"She gave an engaging presentation and spoke confidently throughout it. Her ideas were well organized, and the audience responded positively."

Why It Is Better

  • The vocabulary is more precise.
  • The adverb is used correctly.
  • The strengths are clearly explained.
  • Repetition is reduced.

5. What Makes a Complete Sentence?

A complete sentence needs: A subject, A verb, A complete idea

Complete

The student improved.

  • Subject: the student
  • Verb: improved
  • Complete idea: yes

Incomplete

Because the student improved.

Complete Version

Because the student improved, she became more confident.

6. Basic Sentence Structure

The most common English sentence order is: Subject + Verb + Object

Example: The student completed the assignment.

Additional details may be added: Subject + Verb + Object + Manner + Place + Time

The student completed the assignment carefully at home last night.

Subject: the student
Verb: completed
Object: the assignment
Manner: carefully
Place: at home
Time: last night

7. Building a Sentence Step by Step

  • Start with: I study.
  • Add an object: I study English.
  • Add frequency: I study English every day.
  • Add a place: I study English at home every day.
  • Add a reason: I study English at home every day because I want to become more fluent.
  • Add contrast: Although I am sometimes tired, I study English at home every day because I want to become more fluent.

A longer sentence is not automatically better. Every detail should add useful meaning.

8. The Question Method for Extending Sentences

Begin with: The student practised.

Add information by answering:

  • Who? (The student)
  • What? (English pronunciation)
  • Where? (at home)
  • When? (every evening)
  • How? (by recording her voice)
  • Why? (to sound clearer)
  • What was the result? (confidence improved)
Extended Version

"The student practised English pronunciation at home every evening by recording her voice because she wanted to sound clearer. As a result, her confidence improved."

This method can later be used for: Paragraphs, Essays, Emails, Reports, Articles, Speaking answers.

9. Weak and Strong Sentences

Example 1 (Weak)

I like reading.

Stronger

I enjoy reading historical novels because they help me understand how people lived in the past.

Developed

I particularly enjoy reading historical novels because they help me understand how people lived and thought in the past. For example, they often present historical events through the experiences of ordinary people.

Example 2 (Weak)

Technology is good.

Problems:

  • Good is too general.
  • The benefit is unclear.
  • There is no support.
Strong

Technology can make education more accessible because students can reach lessons and educational materials from different locations. For example, a student who lives far from a university may attend an online course.

Example 3 (Weak)

My job is difficult.

Strong

My job can be challenging because I have to manage several responsibilities at the same time. For instance, I may need to communicate with students, prepare materials, and solve unexpected problems during the same day.

10. Common Sentence Problems

Missing Subject

Incorrect: Is important to practise.

Correct: It is important to practise.

Missing Verb

Incorrect: My teacher very supportive.

Correct: My teacher is very supportive.

Incorrect Word Order

Incorrect: I every day practise English.

Correct: I practise English every day.

Sentence Fragment

Fragment: Because I wanted to improve.

Complete: I joined the course because I wanted to improve.

Run-On Sentence

Incorrect: I wanted to attend the lesson I was feeling unwell.

Correct: I wanted to attend the lesson, but I was feeling unwell.

Wrong Word Form

Incorrect: She answered confidence.

Correct: She answered confidently.

Unnecessary Repetition

Weak: The course was useful because the course gave me useful information.

Improved: The course provided valuable information.

Direct Translation

Unnatural: I made a research.
Natural: I did some research.
Unnatural: I took a decision.
Natural: I made a decision.
Unnatural: She explained me the problem.
Natural: She explained the problem to me.

11. From Strong Sentences to a Short Paragraph

Weak Paragraph

Exercise is useful. People exercise. It is healthy. It is good. People should exercise.

Problems

  • The vocabulary is repetitive.
  • The sentences are disconnected.
  • There is no explanation.
  • There is no example.
  • There is no clear conclusion.

Strong Paragraph

Regular exercise can improve both physical and mental health. It strengthens the body, reduces stress, and can help people sleep better. For example, even a thirty-minute walk may improve a person’s mood and energy level. For these reasons, exercise should be part of a healthy weekly routine.

Strong paragraphs begin with strong sentences.

PART TWO: SPEAKING SKILLS

12. Why Learners Give Short Answers

Question

Do you enjoy learning English?

Short Answer

Yes.

This answer is correct, but it does not create meaningful communication.

Common reasons for short answers include:

  • Fear of mistakes
  • Lack of confidence
  • Translating mentally
  • Trying to create perfect sentences
  • Not knowing how to organize ideas
  • Forgetting reasons and examples
  • Depending on memorized answers

A clear structure helps you speak for longer.

14. The PREP Technique

P

Point

R

Reason

E

Example

P

Point repeated

Use PREP for: Opinion questions, Formal discussions, Longer answers, Presentations, Persuasive speaking.

Question: Is learning English important?

Point: I believe learning English is extremely important.

Reason: The main reason is that it gives people more educational and professional opportunities.

Example: For example, many international companies require employees to communicate in English.

Point Repeated: For this reason, improving English can have a major effect on a person’s future.

Complete Answer: I believe learning English is extremely important. The main reason is that it gives people more educational and professional opportunities. For example, many international companies require employees to communicate in English. For this reason, improving English can have a major effect on a person’s future.

Weak and Strong PREP Answers

Question: Should students use technology in class?

Weak

Yes, because technology is useful and students like it.

Problems:

  • The point is general.
  • Useful is not explained.
  • There is no clear example.
  • There is no final conclusion.

Strong

I believe technology should be used in the classroom when it supports a clear learning purpose. It can help students understand difficult ideas through videos, images, and interactive activities. For example, language learners can listen to authentic pronunciation and practise through online exercises. Therefore, technology can make lessons more effective when it is used appropriately.

15. ARE or PREP?

Use ARE when:

  • The question is simple or personal.
  • The conversation is informal.
  • One reason and one example are enough.

Use PREP when:

  • The question asks for an opinion.
  • The answer needs more organization.
  • You want to persuade or explain.
  • You need a clear conclusion.

16. Extending Spoken Answers Through Questions

Main Question: What is your favourite way to learn English?

Useful planning questions:

  • What is my answer?
  • Why do I prefer it?
  • How does it help?
  • What example can I give?
  • Is there a disadvantage?
  • What is my conclusion?

Keyword Notes:

educational videospronunciation visual supportreplay sometimes passivecombine with speaking

Developed Answer

My favourite way to learn English is by watching educational videos. I prefer this method because I can hear natural pronunciation and use visual information to understand the topic. For example, if I do not understand a sentence, I can replay it. However, watching videos can become passive, so I combine it with speaking and note-taking. Overall, videos are useful when I actively practise what I learn.

17. Speaking from Keywords

Avoid writing a complete script before speaking.

A full script may make you: Read instead of communicate, Sound mechanical, Depend on memorization, Panic when you forget one sentence, Lose natural rhythm.

Use keywords instead.

Topic: Is working from home effective?

Keyword Plan:

  • some jobs
  • saves travel time
  • flexibility
  • parents
  • isolation
  • hybrid system

Developed Answer

I think working from home can be effective, especially for jobs that do not require employees to be physically present. One advantage is that it saves commuting time and gives workers more flexibility. For example, parents may find it easier to organize their responsibilities. However, remote workers can sometimes feel isolated. Overall, I think a hybrid system may provide the best balance.

18. Common Speaking Mistakes

Avoid:

  • Giving only yes-or-no answers
  • Repeating the question without adding information
  • Memorizing every sentence
  • Using difficult vocabulary you cannot control
  • Stopping after every small mistake
  • Repeating the same reason
  • Giving unrelated examples
  • Speaking without a clear point
  • Translating the whole answer before speaking
  • Reading directly from a written paragraph

    • 19. Writing Self-Editing Checklist

    Check:

    Sentence Structure

    • Does every sentence have a subject?
    • Does every sentence have a verb?
    • Is the idea complete?

    Word Forms

    • Do I need a noun, verb, adjective, or adverb?
    • Is the form correct?

    Articles & Pronouns

    • Does the noun need a, an, or the? Am I speaking generally?
    • Is the pronoun clear? Subject/object correct? my vs mine?

    Adjectives, Adverbs & Verbs

    • Am I describing a noun or an action? Is adjective order natural? -ed/-ing correct?
    • Is verb form correct? Base verb after modal? Subject-verb agreement?

    Development & Naturalness

    • Did I explain the idea, give a reason, and an example? Is it relevant?
    • Did I repeat words? Translate directly? Use precise words?
